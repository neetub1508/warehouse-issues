## Scope — why one task and not three

### Adopted solution and configuration contract — finalized

The regulated pack is an optional v2 capability, disabled until a validated profile passes licence, expiry, dispatch/hold, recall and evidence acceptance. NONE leaves regulated workflows unavailable; it is not a claim of compliance. Build the declared registry/guards in this task, not a generic legal rules engine. The product decision is adopted; historical demands for another yes/no decision are superseded.

Apply [Global settings and resolved behaviour](https://github.com/neetub1508/warehouse-issues/blob/main/docs/GLOBAL-SETTINGS-DECISIONS.md) only within this task’s existing module/version/migration ownership. P0-15/P1-20 own the shared settings implementation; this task consumes it. Do not build unrelated settings controls or later-version capabilities here.

A licence regime is a licence object, an expiry clock, a despatch guard and a register, and splitting
them produces three tables that each need the other two. What follows is **one** pack that serves
pharma, food, liquor, pesticides, fertilisers and explosives, because they differ in the fields and
the ceiling, not in the shape.

1. **`whin_licence_types` — an open registry of regimes.** Drug, narcotic, explosive, pesticide,
   fertiliser, liquor, arms. Each row names the fields its licence carries and whether it gates
   despatch. A registry row, never a `CHECK` value (`L-4`).
2. **`whin_entity_licences` — our own licence as an object with an expiry clock.** Number, issuing
   authority, validity window, the site it is scoped to, and the renewal alert through
   `whb_alert_rules`. FSSAI already requires this for every food business operator; the drug licence
   requires it per premises.
3. **`whin_counterparty_licences` — the counterparty's licence**, which is the half that actually
   blocks a despatch. *No valid drug licence on the consignee, no despatch of a scheduled drug to
   them.* This is the row `S-035` calls the hard guard, and it is why the pack cannot be a report.
4. **`whin_licence_quantity_ceilings`** for the regimes that cap quantity per period rather than
   merely permitting the trade — narcotics and some pesticides — with the running consumption against
   the ceiling.
5. **`whin_schedule_h1_register` — an output of the ledger, not a parallel book** (`FR-457`). It is
   *built* from despatch movements of H1-classified items with the prescriber and patient fields the
   rule names, as a run that can be rebuilt. The same discipline `P4-09` applies to Form 3CD.
6. **`whin_recall_notifications` — the regulator-facing half of a recall.** Who was notified, when,
   under what reference. **The affected-stock and affected-shipment halves already exist** as
   `wh_recalls` and `wh_recall_lines` (`P5-14`) and are not duplicated here.

**What this task does not build.** It does not implement DSCSA or the EU FMD. `S-035`'s own verdict is
that we implement *the shape* — SGTIN identity, aggregation, and an event log with EPCIS's four
dimensions — so that an adapter can emit either; the identity half is `P3-24` and the event log is the
ledger. Nothing here changes `warehouse-base`.

## Scenarios closed

- A despatch of a Schedule H drug to a consignee whose drug licence expired yesterday is **refused**,
  naming the licence and its expiry.
- The same despatch, to the same consignee, succeeds the day the renewed licence is recorded.
- Our own FSSAI licence enters its renewal window and raises the alert; nothing is blocked yet.
- Our own licence expires and despatch of the regulated class stops at that site — and only at that
  site.
- A narcotics despatch that would breach the period ceiling is refused with the remaining quantity
  stated.
- The Schedule H1 register for a month is built, and rebuilt, from the same movements and produces the
  same rows.
- A recall raised on a lot quarantines stock (`P5-14`) and records the regulator notification here.
- The ids for these are authored into `SCENARIO-CATALOGUE.md` in the same PR.

## Traps

- **The guard belongs on the counterparty licence, not on ours.** Our licence lets us trade at all;
  theirs lets us trade *with them*. A pack that only models our own licence passes every test and
  blocks nothing.
- **The regulated class lives on the item, and it forces lot control.** An item marked as a scheduled
  drug with `lot_control_mode = NONE` is an unshippable contradiction; refuse it at the item screen
  rather than at the despatch.
- **Expiry is evaluated at despatch, against the despatch date** — not at order entry, and not against
  `now()` at report time. A licence valid when the order was taken and expired when the truck loads is
  the case the guard exists for.
- **The register is a run, not a table that is appended to as movements post.** A parallel book drifts
  from the ledger and the drift is discovered by an inspector. `P4-09`'s Form 3CD run/line pair is the
  pattern.
- **Do not duplicate `wh_recalls`.** The stock half is the app's, in v2, and it already quarantines in
  place and lists every shipment that carried the lot. This task adds the notification trail only.
- **Rule numbers are unverified.** `S-035` states the Drugs & Cosmetics Act 1940 / Drugs Rules 1945
  with *"rule numbers unverified"*, and `F-073` marks the export track-and-trace obligations `?`. The
  schema is safe because it is generic; **the seeded `whin_licence_types` rows and the H1 field list
  must be confirmed with a regulatory adviser before the seed migration is written**, and that
  confirmation is part of this task's definition of done.
- **This is `warehouse-india`, not an adapter.** `S-035` proposed `adapter-pharma` and `F-073`
  proposed `warehouse-adapter-pharma`; neither exists in `DATA-MODEL.md` §2.5's five adapters, and a
  licence with a statutory expiry is a localisation, not a vertical join. Stated rather than diverged
  from silently.

## Decisions recorded at build
W3 gate round (reviewer clean; functional-reviewer F1–F9), ratified by the driver:
- **The guard covers drop-shipment** (F1). `WhDropShipService#confirm` asks every `WhDropShipGuard` bean (a warehouse port that mirrors `WhShipmentDispatchGuard`) after its own validations and before `ledgerWriter.post`. `WhinRegulatedGoodsDispatchGuard` implements both ports. It converts the confirmed quantity to the item's base unit through `WhbUomConversionResolver`, and fails closed when a capping type has no conversion. Ceiling consumption and the H1 source now also count `DROP_SHIP` / `DROP_SHIP_REVERSAL`: the customer line, reached through `source_line_ref` → demand line → order, counts positive. **Contract amendment:** the despatch guard fires on the dispatch path AND on the drop-ship confirm.
- **A regulated despatch needs a registered consignee** (F2). An order with no `customer_counterparty_id` is refused `COUNTERPARTY_LICENCE_REQUIRED` when the item's type gates or caps. The exception is a demand type that stays inside the enterprise: `TRANSFER`, `REPLENISHMENT` or `SCRAP` is checked against our own licence only. **WH-SC-389 amendment:** add the case "a SALES order with no consignee is refused; a TRANSFER passes on our licence".
- **Ceilings survive a renewal** (F3). The guard reads the item's live ceilings on **every** active licence of the type that the counterparty holds, and applies those whose licence validity overlaps the ceiling's current period. A capping type with no live consignee licence now fails closed; before, it skipped the ceilings. No migration: ceilings stay keyed on the licence row. **WH-SC-391 amendment:** add the case "a renewed licence still carries the FY ceiling recorded on its predecessor".
- **One day convention** (F4). Consumption and H1 lines are bucketed by the day the movement occurred at its site: `CAST(occurred_at AT TIME ZONE site timezone AS DATE)`. `posting_date` stays only as an outer fence, ±1 day. The H1 line's `posting_date` column holds that site day. **Apply-to (recorded, not fixed here):** P4-09's Form 3CD build and the other India readers of `posting_date`. The root cause is core `WhDespatchService` posting a UTC date.
- **Ceiling checks are serialised** (F5). `pg_advisory_xact_lock` on (counterparty, item) is taken before consumption is read, inside the dispatch or drop-ship transaction. **Recorded:** one dispatch can lock several (counterparty, item) pairs in fact order, so two dispatches over the same pairs in opposite orders can deadlock. PostgreSQL aborts one of them and the user retries.
- **Recorded, not fixed** (F6). The item-screen refusal of an unlotted regulated item, and the H1 prescriber and patient fields (adviser-gated), move to the follow-up #188. Their acceptance boxes above stay **unchecked**. The despatch-time `REGULATED_ITEM_NOT_LOT_CONTROLLED` check remains the interim.
- **The refusal names a retired licence** (F7). `findLatestLicence` (ours and the consignee's) no longer filters `is_active`, so the refusal says "…was retired" (WH-SC-390 as written).
- **The H1 rebuild is stable against a licence retirement** (F8). The consignee licence is matched on validity alone, because the EXCLUDE already makes it unique. **Recorded:** the H1 classification and the counterparty's code and name are read as of the build.
- **A ceiling must match the item's type** (F9). Create and reactivation refuse `CEILING_ITEM_TYPE_MISMATCH` when the item's `REGULATED_LICENCE_TYPE` is not the licence's type.
- **Recorded (parity):** the other India dispatch guards (e-way bill, transfer IRN, bonded transfer) do not implement `WhDropShipGuard`. Whether a drop-shipment needs an e-way bill is a separate decision.
- **Contract quality (recorded):** the body's "NONE leaves regulated workflows unavailable" is superseded by the W3 header. Under `NONE`, the screens, renewal alert and register stay available and only the guard is inert. Ceilings summed across the orders of one dispatch are part of WH-SC-391.
