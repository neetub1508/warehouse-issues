## Scope

> **Round-2 addition — `Z-006`.** **The retention mechanism is base; India seeds it.** The
> retention **table** is `warehouse-base` — **`whb_retention_policies`** (scope, clock kind,
> duration, legal-hold flag), allocated `WHB-69` / `V500067`, with a **country-neutral default of
> the longest applicable statutory period**. `warehouse-india` seeds the two Indian clocks into it
> and adds the GST-specific report. India owns the clocks it knows about, **not the mechanism** —
> otherwise a non-Indian install has no retention at all, and the second country to arrive has to
> move the table.

- **`V540170`** — `whin_retention_policies` (`DATA-MODEL.md` §7.6 WIN-20).
- **Two retention clocks run on the same rows** (`FR-329`): the **Companies Act's financial years**
  for books of account and the **GST period**. Food adds a third, **per-item** clock — *one year or
  the shelf life, whichever is longer*. So retention is a **policy object with a legal-hold flag**,
  not a global setting.
- **Retention beats erasure, satisfied by pseudonymising the person and never the quantity**
  (`FR-441`). Stated once, here, so the precedence question is not re-litigated per screen.
- **The tax-audit quantitative statement as a built report** (`FR-329`): the **s.44AB / Form 3CD**
  per-item quantitative reconciliation for a financial year — opening, purchases, sales, closing and
  shortage/excess — that must **tie to both the stock ledger and the GL**.
- **Archival must preserve "as at" reproduction rather than delete** (`FR-023` is v3; this task sets
  the rule the v3 archiver has to honour).

## Traps

- **Retention beats erasure, and the resolution is pseudonymisation.** A DPDP erasure request against
  a warehouse ledger cannot delete the quantity — the movement is a statutory record. **The person is
  pseudonymised; the quantity never changes.** Stated once here; a screen that re-argues it has
  already got it wrong.
- **Pseudonymising an actor must not break the audit hash chain.** `whb_audit_events` is append-only
  and **hash-chained** (`P0-13`, the `accounting-base/V600111` template). Rewriting an actor id in
  place invalidates every subsequent hash, and the verifier will name *"the break between events 59
  and 61"*. The pseudonym must be a **mapping**, applied at read, not an update to the chained row.
- **Three clocks, and the longest wins.** A row under a GST clock of 72 months, a Companies Act clock
  of 8 financial years and a shelf-life clock is retained until the **latest** of the three. An
  implementation that stores one `retain_until` computed at write time is wrong the moment a policy
  changes — the resolution belongs in a query over the policy object.
- **A legal hold beats every clock**, including an expired one, and its release is a permissioned,
  audited action.
- **Archival preserves "as at" reproduction rather than deletes** (`FR-328`, `S-096`, `WH-SC-253`).
  A 1M-rows/day ledger needs a partition or archive strategy that does **not** break the ability to
  recompute a past position — and note `IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN.md` §11 item 1: **the ledger's partition
  key is still unresolved** between `occurred_at` (`FR-022`, `DATA-MODEL.md` WHB-30) and
  `posting_date` (`PLATFORM-DEPENDENCIES.md` `PD-D5`). That choice constrains this rule and this task
  must not choose it either.
- **Form 3CD ties to *both* the stock ledger and the GL.** A quantitative statement that reconciles
  to one and not the other is the number an auditor will find. The GL side arrives through the
  handover envelope and `posting_status` (`D-6`), not by reading `acc_*` — **warehouse never reads or
  writes an `acc_*` table**, and an architecture test fails the build if any class under
  `ai.warehouse*` references one.
- **Financial years are not calendar years and are not the same as GST periods.** Three period
  vocabularies, three sets of boundaries; a single `period_id` shared between them is how a return
  gets filed for the wrong twelve months.
- **Every retention date is a SQL `DATE`** (`FR-327`).
- **Retention is `warehouse-india` policy over `warehouse-base` rows**, and base gains no FK to a
  `whin_` table (`D-11`). The base half of `FR-441` is the pseudonymisation mechanism; the India half
  is the clocks.

## Acceptance
- [ ] Three clock kinds exist and a row's retention resolves to the **longest** applicable, computed at query time from the policy object
- [ ] A per-item shelf-life override lengthens retention for food items
- [ ] A legal hold overrides an expired clock, and its release is permissioned and audited
- [ ] An erasure request pseudonymises the person and leaves every quantity, movement and lot untouched
- [ ] Pseudonymisation does **not** rewrite a chained audit row, and the hash-chain verifier still passes end to end afterwards
- [ ] The Form 3CD quantitative statement produces opening, purchases, sales, closing and shortage/excess per item for a financial year
- [ ] It ties to the stock ledger and to the GL through the handover envelope, and no warehouse class references an `acc_*` table
- [ ] Financial years, GST periods and shelf-life windows are three distinct period vocabularies
- [ ] Archival deletes nothing that "as at" reproduction needs
- [ ] Every retention value carries a RE-VERIFY marker and every section citation is marked `UNVERIFIED`
- [ ] **Scenarios** — none claimed; see the coverage note above
