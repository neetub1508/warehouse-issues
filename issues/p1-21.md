## Scope — the day-two problem the import task creates on day one

`P1-16` loads a 40,000-SKU catalogue against `uk(owner_id, sku)`. **Duplicates are not a risk, they are
the expected outcome**: the incumbent's file carries the same physical part twice under two SKUs, or
carries a supplier under two names, and no import validation can tell a genuine second part from a
typo. Round 2 found that the set has **no merge path at all** (`Z-007`), and the consequence is
specific: the implementation team will do it in `psql`, against an **append-only ledger with no
`DELETE`** (`L-2`), and the first month-end after that is unreconcilable.

This task builds the merge as an ordinary, auditable operation:

1. **A pre-check screen listing everything that references the loser.** Positions, open reservations,
   open documents, identifiers, external refs, supersession chains, supplier sources, site and location
   settings. A merge that surprises the operator is a merge that gets reversed by hand.
2. **For an item that holds stock, a transfer to the survivor posted as a real movement through the
   port** — `POST /api/warehouse/movements`, a dedicated reason code, one balanced pair of lines per
   position (`L-1`). **History is never re-pointed.** `L-2` forbids it and `I-` forbids it, and the
   distinction is the whole task: the ledger keeps saying the loser existed and held stock, because it
   did.
3. **Deactivation of the loser with a scan redirect.** Its barcodes and identifiers keep resolving —
   to the survivor — because the labels are already on the shelf and reprinting 40,000 of them is not a
   migration plan. The redirect is `whb_item_identifiers` rows re-parented to the survivor with the
   merge recorded, not deleted.
4. **An explicit refusal** where the two rows differ in `base_uom_code`, `lot_control_mode` or
   `serial_control_mode`. These are not reconcilable by a transfer: merging a lot-controlled item into
   an uncontrolled one destroys the lot dimension of every future balance, and merging across base UoM
   makes the transferred quantity a different physical amount. The refusal is `409` with the differing
   column named.
5. **`whb_master_merges` as the record**, with `uk(entity_type, losing_id)` — a row can only lose once,
   which is what stops a merge chain becoming a cycle.

**`WONTFIX` for v1 is a legitimate answer to this task. Silence is not** — that is why it exists as a
file rather than as a line in a review.

## Scenarios closed
- **Two duplicate items, one holding stock**, merged: the pre-check lists the positions, the transfer
  posts as a balanced movement pair, the loser goes inactive, its barcode resolves to the survivor, and
  the ledger still shows the loser's original receipt.
- **Two duplicate items with different `lot_control_mode`**, merge refused with the column named.
- **Two duplicate counterparties**, one with an open PO: the pre-check lists the PO, the merge either
  refuses or re-points the *open document* (a decision this task states in its own PR) — and whichever
  it picks, `whb_master_merges` records it.

## Traps
- **This is not `whb_item_supersessions`.** A supersession is a true commercial fact about two real
  parts — the OEM replaced A with B — and it is queried forever. A merge says **one of them never
  existed**. Recording a merge as a supersession pollutes the interchange chain that `FR-176` allocates
  from, and `P1-08` will happily allocate the loser's stock against a real order.
- **Never `UPDATE whb_stock_movement_lines SET item_id = …`.** It is the one operation this task exists
  to prevent, and it is the one the team will reach for. Assert it in the PR: `grep` the migration and
  the service for an `UPDATE` against any ledger table.
- **Every position moves, including the ones in non-`AVAILABLE` statuses and in virtual locations.**
  A merge that only moves available stock leaves quarantined and in-transit units pointing at a
  deactivated item, which is the same orphan the merge was supposed to remove.
- **Open reservations against the loser are released before the transfer, not carried across.** A
  reservation holder quad names the loser; re-pointing it silently is the same class of edit as
  re-pointing history.
- **The merge is not a bulk operation in v1.** One loser, one survivor, one confirmation. A
  "merge all duplicates" button over an append-only ledger is how a bad match becomes 400 bad matches.
- **`whb_audit_events` records it too**, because `whb_master_merges` says *what* and the audit trail
  says *who and from where* — and a merge is exactly the operation an auditor asks about.
