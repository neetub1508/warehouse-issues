`P0-09` built the reservation as an **open-item ledger row**, never a counter: the holder quad
`(holder_system, holder_document_type, holder_document_id, holder_line_no)`, `expires_at`,
`DELETE /reservations?holder=…`, and availability computed at read time and never stored. This task
builds the **allocator** on top of it.

**Soft and hard are distinct, and the Release action exists in v1 even though the wave does not**
(`FR-169`, `FR-187`). Release is the moment soft becomes hard and pick tasks are created. *Without
it every order hard-allocates at creation, a cancelled order's stock stays locked and one shortage
blocks another.* v1 ships **Release on the demand header** (`P2-08`'s WS-099/WS-100); `wh_waves`
and the wave object are `P3-06`. **The deferral is stated, not silent.**
A soft reservation **yields** to a competing demand under priority; a hard one does not.

**`SOFT` reservations expire; `HARD` ones do not** (`FR-170`, as amended by `RJ-007`).
- **`expires_at` applies to `SOFT` rows only.** A scheduled job releases them, **notifies the holder**,
  writes a **movement-free audit row** — no ledger event, because no stock moved — and feeds an ageing
  report. *A part held against an abandoned estimate is invisible dead stock.*
- **A `HARD` row ends only by consume, cancel or an explicit release.** Before round 4 the job
  released a released order's hold mid-pick, or while the stock sat in staging. That reached
  `Y-003`'s orphaned-staging state through a job instead of a cancel.
- **The ageing report lists stale `HARD` rows in their own section**, so a stuck hold is seen rather
  than silently released (`WH-SC-317`).

**The allocator's hold check is one query over open `wh_holds`** (`RJ-008`). A lot hold is a
`wh_holds` row (`P2-03`). The allocator never reads `whb_lots.status_code` as a hold. An expired lot
reaches it through the `EXPIRED` stock status, which the allocator refuses by that status's own flag.

**De-allocation on cancel is deterministic and reason-coded** (`FR-171`), cancels un-started tasks,
and carries a **stated acceptance test**: allocate ten, cancel, and availability returns to
**exactly** its pre-allocation value.

**Strategy is a configured row, not an `if`-statement** (`FR-172`). `whb_allocation_strategies` rows
carry a **whitelisted ordering key** — `FIFO`, `FEFO`, `LIFO`, lot-specific, nearest location,
fewest locations, single-LPN-preferred, highest-quantity bin — and `whb_allocation_strategy_rules`
resolve **most-specific-first** by site, item, owner, customer and demand type. v1 seeds
`FIFO-BY-RECEIPT` and fixed-location-first. *Hard-coding FIFO makes FEFO a branch and the allocator
untestable by v2* (`IRR-47`).

**The rule and strategy that chose the stock are recorded on the reservation row and shown on its
detail view** (`FR-173`) — `allocation_strategy_id`, `allocation_rule_id`, `chosen_reason`.
*"Why did the system pick lot B when lot A expires sooner" is asked weekly.*

**A rule row that a reservation references is immutable** (`RL-010`, `I-24`). Before round 4 rules
were edited in place, so the ids a reservation recorded pointed at rules that had since changed.
- An edit to a strategy, a strategy rule or an allocation rule is **copy-on-write**. The new row
  carries `version_no` and `supersedes_id`, the old row stays, and a reservation keeps naming the
  version that chose.
- `P0-09` puts the columns and `I-24`'s trigger in `V500033`.
- **WS-048 shows each row's version history**, so *"which rule was live when this was allocated"* is
  one click.

**`demand_type_code` reads the `DEMAND_TYPE` code list** (`RL-006`), through a composite FK, not a
`CHECK`. `TRANSFER` and `VENDOR_RETURN` are rows of that list (`P2-08`), so allocation resolves for
them exactly as for a sale.

**`item_category_id` in the rule tuple is the item's `STOCKING` category** (`RG-005`). It is resolved
from `whb_item_category_assignments` (`P1-01`) as current at the demand's allocation. A
re-categorisation changes future allocation, and never the reason already recorded on a reservation.

**A first-class availability API, single and bulk** (`FR-174`), returning on-hand, hard-allocated,
soft-allocated, available, in-transit, on-order and earliest expiry, scoped by item, site, owner and
as-of date. *Before any vertical wants waves it wants "can I promise this part today", and a
quotation screen asks for forty items at once.*

**Supersession-aware allocation under an explicit rule flag** (`FR-176`): where the requested item
is short, the allocator may consult the supersession chain, and reporting distinguishes *"stock of
A"* from *"stock of A including superseded equivalents"*. The chain itself is `P1-03`'s
`whb_item_supersessions`; the **treatment** (`KEEP_SEPARATE`/`MERGE_DEMAND`/`MERGE_STOCK`) is
`P2-24`.

**WS-048 · Allocation Strategies** · `/warehouse/masters/allocation-strategies` · Department
reference · scope `WAREHOUSE_ALLOCATION_STRATEGY`. Columns `code`, `name`, `owningModule`,
`scopeType`, `scopeRef`, `priority`, `orderingKey`, `isActive`; child editor over the rule rows.
**No `CHECK` on `ordering_key`** — it is a whitelist enforced by the allocator, exposed as a
catalogue-backed dropdown that fetches (`D-10`, `OD-5`).

**Mobile.** `none` for WS-048 (a master a controller configures), stated. The reservation surface a
picker sees is `WS-047`'s (`P0-09`) and the RF pick screen (`P3`).

## Traps

- **`C-022` — reservations in this codebase are schema-only.** `accessory_stock_levels.quantity_reserved`
  is read in 7 places and **written in 0**; `StockLevel.reserveQuantity()` and
  `releaseReservedQuantity()` (`StockLevel.java:189-206`) have **zero call sites**.
  **Do not assume any prior art exists.** This is net-new.
- **Availability is computed, never stored** (`P0-03`, `WH-SC-188`):
  `available = Σ on_hand where status.is_allocatable − Σ open reservations`. A stored
  `quantity_available` is the second truth that produces `C-024`'s race.
- **A released reservation is never `DELETE`d** — `released_at` is set and the row stays
  (`WH-SC-185`). *The history of who held what is the point.*
- **`IRR-47` — strategy is data.** *Nothing historical is lost if you hardcode it, which is why it is
  reliably the first thing cut for time and the cut is never revisited.* The whitelist is bounded
  rows, **not** an expression language and **not** a JSONB condition bag (`FR-383`).
- **The expiry job is a dated obligation with three parts** (`FR-165`): the job, the notification to
  the **holder** (who may be another module, identified only by the holder quad — so the notify
  path must resolve a holder to a recipient through the display-resolver registry, `FR-357`), and
  the ageing report. Registered in `P1-20` with the expiry and count jobs.
- **The reservation-expiry release writes no movement.** It is an audit row. A movement would break
  `L-4`'s rebuild, because nothing physically moved.
- **`WH-SC-190`/`WH-SC-197` are `P0-03`'s, and they constrain this task.** Allocation must hit
  200 order lines a second **with no oversell**; the conflict is resolved at **allocation**, not at
  pick (`WH-SC-191`). Two simultaneous issues of the last unit: exactly one succeeds.
- **`T-6`** — `WAREHOUSE_ALLOCATION_STRATEGY` into `COMMON_FILTER_CONFIGS`. The availability API is
  not a grid and needs no scope, but it **does** need `@PreAuthorize` on every method.
- **`OD-5`** — the ordering-key dropdown fetches; `mobile/src/schemas/common.schemas.ts` must not
  re-enumerate it as a zod enum.
- **Precision.** `quantity`, `base_quantity` `DECIMAL(18,4)`; there is no money column on a
  reservation.
