PRD — Manufacturing, Purchase, Stores & Quality Module
Oct 24, 2026 · @Samir kumar
1. Overview
This module adds purchase, stores, quality and production to Voyager CRM, so one system carries a vehicle from material requirement to the sales invoice. Every document moves only when an approved user acts on it.
Problem. Today sales (leads, quotations), service (warranty, Tally import) and the item master live in the CRM, but buying, receiving, inspecting, issuing and building happen on paper, in Excel or in Tally. Nobody can answer "which battery serial went into chassis X, who inspected it, and which PO bought it" without hunting.
Goals
	•	One traceable chain: PR → PO → Gate Entry → IQC → MRN → Store → Material Requisition → Issue → Production → Process QC → PDI → Invoice → Payment.
	•	Production driven by the Sales Order, built against an approved BOM and routing, batch-wise and stage-wise.
	•	No stock movement without a document, and no document without an approved user.
	•	Samples and drawings handled against the SO, with drawing approval before any sample is built.
	•	Serial and batch traceability that feeds the existing Warranty & Service registry.
Out of scope (v1): direct filing on government portals in v1 (the system prepares the return data and upload files; §12A is the complete accounts module), payroll, MRP auto-planning across many SOs, and vendor portal logins.

2. End-to-end flow

end-to-end flow · enquiry to payment, 20 documents
The approved Sales Order is the trigger: it raises the Production Order, whose BOM drives the material requisition. Free stock goes straight to issue; a shortage runs through purchase and inward (right to left) until an MRN puts accepted stock in the Main store. IQC rejects go back to the vendor (§6), PQC and PDI failures go to rework (§9–10), and every box is a document only an approved user can approve.
3. Approved users and approvals (applies to every module)
A document can be created, approved, or acted on only by a user who holds the permission for that action, and no user approves their own document. This is the rule behind requirement 11: sample production, drawings and every stock movement work only on approved users.
Rules
	•	Permission per action, not per screen: pr:create, pr:approve, po:approve, iqc:inspect, mrn:approve, issue:post, bom:release, drawing:approve, prodorder:release, pdi:approve, and so on. Roles bundle them; Admin → Roles edits them.
	•	Maker ≠ checker. The creator of a document can never approve it. Enforced in the service, not hidden in the UI.
	•	Value limits. Each approver has a limit per document type (e.g. PO up to ₹1,00,000). Above it, the document routes to the next level (Purchase Manager → Plant Head → MD). Limit used is recorded on the approval.
	•	Multi-level approval matrix, configurable per document type, per value band, per item category, per plant/store.
	•	Approved status locks the document. Change after approval = an amendment (new revision, re-approval), never an edit in place. Revision history is kept.
	•	Reason mandatory on reject, cancel, short-close, amendment, deviation and override.
	•	Approved vendor list, approved BOM, approved drawing. A PO can only go to an approved vendor for that item; production can only run on a released BOM + routing; a sample can only be built from an approved drawing revision.
	•	Authorised operators per stage. Each routing stage lists who may book output on it (skill/operator master). QC stages list authorised inspectors.
	•	Audit. Every create, approve, reject, post, amend and print writes audit_log with before/after, user and time. Approvals are append-only.
	•	Notifications. In-app (and email where configured) to the next approver; overdue approvals escalate after a configurable number of hours.
Approval statuses (common to all documents): Draft → Submitted → Approved (L1…Ln) → Released/Posted → Closed; or Rejected (back to Draft for revision) / Cancelled (with reason).

4A. CRM before the Sales Order
No Sales Order exists without a CRM trail behind it: a lead or customer, an enquiry, an approved quotation, and the customer's PO or confirmation. The existing CRM already covers leads, calls, follow-ups, dealers and approved quotations; this section adds what is missing between an approved quotation and a manufacturing SO.
	•	Lead / Customer (existing). Lead from Meta, manual or dealer; customer master with GSTIN, billing and shipping addresses, credit limit, payment terms. A lead converts to a customer account when the first SO is booked.
	•	Enquiry / Opportunity (new). Against the lead or customer: product/model, variant, colour, qty, required delivery date, special requirements, customer drawing or spec (attachments), standard or custom product, sample needed (Y/N).
	•	Technical feasibility (new, only for custom/non-standard). Design/R&D confirms feasibility, BOM availability, new drawing needed, tooling, estimated lead time. Approved by Design Head before costing.
	•	Costing / price approval (new, custom items). Material cost from BOM × last purchase rate, labour/routing cost, overhead, margin; below-floor price needs approval from Sales Manager / MD.
	•	Quotation (existing). Draft → Pending approval → Approved → Sent; revision kept; watermark and PDF as today. Gains a validity date, delivery period, payment terms and a link to the enquiry.
	•	Sample request (if needed) — goes to §11 before the order is confirmed.
	•	Customer PO / Order confirmation (new). Customer PO no., date, copy upload, advance received (amount, UTR). Order acceptance checks: price = approved quotation, credit limit, overdue outstanding (from Tally), stock/production availability.
	•	Sales Order (new). Created from the approved quotation (lines copied, not retyped). Fields: SO no., customer, billing/shipping address, PO ref., lines (item/model, variant, colour, qty, rate, GST, delivery schedule per line), dispatch mode, freight terms, special instructions, drawing revision for custom items.
	•	Approval: Sales Manager; credit-limit or price exception → Accounts / MD.
	•	On approval: stock check per line → reserve FG stock if available; for the balance, a Production Order is suggested (§9) and BOM shortages raise PRs (§5).
	•	Status: Draft → Approved → In production → Partly dispatched → Fully dispatched → Invoiced → Closed; Cancel / short-close with reason.
	•	Amendment: qty, date or spec change after approval = new revision, re-approval, and impact shown on linked production orders.
CRM screens to add: Enquiry list, Feasibility queue, Order book (SO-wise pending qty vs. produced vs. dispatched), Customer 360 (leads, quotations, SOs, samples, invoices, service claims).
5. Purchase — with or without Purchase Requisition
A PO can be raised against one or more approved PRs, or directly without a PR when the user holds po:create_without_pr; both paths go through the same PO approval.
5.1 Purchase Requisition (PR)
	•	Sources: manual (any department), auto from shortage (BOM requirement for a production order vs. free stock), auto from reorder level, from a Material Requisition that the store cannot fulfil.
	•	Fields: PR no., date, department, requested by, required-by date, purpose (Production order / SO / Maintenance / Sample / Stock), reference (Prod. order no., SO no.), lines: item, qty, UoM, required date, suggested vendor, last purchase rate, current stock, remarks.
	•	Flow: Draft → Submitted → HOD approved → Purchase approved → Open → Partly ordered → Fully ordered / Short-closed (reason).
	•	A PR line can be split across several POs; PR balance = approved qty − ordered qty.
5.2 RFQ and comparison (optional)
	•	RFQ to 1–n approved vendors from PR lines; enter quotes; comparison sheet (rate, GST, freight, delivery days, payment terms, last price); select vendor with reason if not L1.
5.3 Purchase Order (PO)
	•	With PR: pull approved PR lines (one or many PRs, same or different departments); qty cannot exceed PR balance unless the user holds po:exceed_pr (tolerance % configurable).
	•	Without PR: allowed only for permitted users and optionally only for listed categories (e.g. consumables, urgent spares); reason mandatory; flagged "Direct PO" in reports.
	•	Fields: vendor (approved for each item), PO type (Standard, Job-work, Service, Sample, Rate contract/Open), delivery schedule per line (multiple dates), rate, discount, GST (CGST/SGST or IGST by place of supply), freight, P&F, terms, payment terms, delivery address, attachments (drawing, spec).
	•	Approval: value-based matrix (§3); PO number assigned on approval; PO PDF on company letterhead; email/WhatsApp share to vendor.
	•	Amendment: revision no. increments; re-approval; old revision kept; cannot reduce qty below received qty.
	•	Status: Draft → Pending approval → Approved → Partly received → Fully received → Closed / Short-closed.
	•	Tolerance: over-receipt allowed up to configurable % per item/category.
	•	Rate contract: price fixed for a period; release orders against it.
	•	Job-work PO: material sent out on a returnable challan, received back as processed goods; tracks material lying with vendor.
5.4 Vendor follow-up
	•	Pending PO list by due date; overdue highlighted; vendor-wise pending; expected arrivals for the week.
6. Inward: Gate Entry → Stock Inward → IQC → MRN → Store Inward
Material enters usable stock only after it passes IQC and an approved MRN posts it to a store; until then it sits in Quarantine and cannot be issued.
	•	Gate Entry (Security). Vehicle no., driver, transporter, LR no., vendor, vendor invoice/challan no. + date, PO no. (one or more), no. of packages, gross weight, in-time, photo of invoice and vehicle. Inward types: Against PO, Job-work return, Customer return, Returnable (our material back), Non-PO (samples, free-of-cost). Gate entry without PO only for non-PO types. Gate pass number generated; out-time recorded when the vehicle leaves.
	•	Stock Inward / GRN (Store). Against the gate entry: pick PO lines, enter invoice qty, received qty (counted), short/excess, damaged qty, batch/lot no., mfg/expiry date, serial nos. (scan or import for serial-tracked items such as battery, motor, controller, charger). Received qty goes to Quarantine (QC Hold) store. Items with QC required = No go straight to MRN.
	•	IQC (Quality). Auto-raised for every GRN line needing QC. Inspector records sample size (AQL plan), each parameter's reading vs. spec, pass/fail, photos, test certificate (COA) from vendor. Result per line: Accepted qty, Rejected qty, Accepted under deviation (needs QC Manager / Plant Head approval with reason). Serial-tracked items can be inspected per serial.
	•	MRN — Material Receipt Note. Created from IQC result (or GRN for non-QC items). Shows PO rate, accepted qty, value. Store Manager approves; on approval accepted qty moves from Quarantine to the Main store and becomes free stock. MRN is the document Purchase/Accounts use for bill booking (export to Tally).
	•	Store Inward / Put-away. Store keeper assigns bin/rack; labels printed with item, batch, serial, MRN no., QR/barcode. Stock ledger updated.
Rejections. Rejected qty moves to the Rejected store; a Rejection Note / Debit Note request is generated against the vendor; material goes back on a Returnable/Non-returnable gate pass (Purchase Return). Replacement received later comes in as a new gate entry against the same PO.
Controls
	•	Cannot receive more than PO balance + tolerance; cannot receive against an unapproved or closed PO.
	•	Duplicate vendor invoice no. for the same vendor is blocked.
	•	IQC cannot be done by the person who made the GRN.
	•	Every movement writes the stock ledger with document no., batch, serial, store, user, time.
7. Material Requisition, Issue and Return
Store issues material only against an approved Material Requisition (MR), and any material coming back to store needs an approved Material Return before it is taken into stock.
7.1 Material Requisition (MR)
	•	Types: Against Production Order (from BOM, §8.4), Against SO sample, Maintenance/General, Consumables, Replacement (extra issue for rejection/scrap at a stage).
	•	Fields: MR no., date, department, requested by, production order / SO / job card ref., stage, required date, lines: item, required qty, UoM, batch preference, remarks.
	•	Flow: Draft → Submitted → Approved (HOD/Production Manager) → Partly issued → Fully issued / Short-closed.
	•	Extra issue beyond BOM requirement needs a reason code and a higher approval.
	•	If free stock is short, the store can raise a PR from the MR in one click (§5.1).
7.2 Material Issue
	•	Store picks approved MR lines; system suggests batches by FIFO/FEFO and shows bin location.
	•	Issued qty ≤ approved balance and ≤ free stock; serial-tracked items need each serial scanned.
	•	Stock moves Main store → WIP (against the production order) or to the consuming department.
	•	Issue slip printed; receiver acknowledges (name/sign or OTP on app).
	•	Issue against MR only — no direct issue without MR, except emergency issue by a permitted user, which auto-creates the MR for post-approval.
7.3 Material Return (with approval)
	•	Reasons: Excess / unused, Wrong issue, Defective found in process (line rejection), Scrap, Production order closed with balance.
	•	Fields: return no., production order / MR / issue ref., item, batch, serial, qty, reason, condition (Good / Defective / Scrap), photos.
	•	Flow: Raised by production → Approved by Production Manager → Store verifies physical qty → Store Manager approves → Material inward in store.
	•	Where it lands: Good → Main store (free stock); Defective → Quarantine for QC decision (use / return to vendor / scrap); Scrap → Scrap store.
	•	Defective bought-out parts can raise a vendor rejection (links back to the original MRN and PO for vendor rating).
	•	Production order consumption is reduced by the returned qty, so actual-vs-BOM variance stays correct.
8. BOM — Component BOM, Routing BOM, MR against BOM
Every producible item has one released component BOM (what goes in) and one released routing (how and where it is made); production uses only released revisions.
8.1 Component BOM
	•	Header: BOM no., parent item (FG or sub-assembly), revision, base qty (e.g. 1 vehicle), effective from/to, drawing no. + revision, status (Draft → Under review → Released → Obsolete).
	•	Lines: component item, qty per base, UoM, scrap/wastage %, item type (RM / bought-out / sub-assembly), issue stage (which routing stage consumes it), alternate items (with priority), serial-tracked flag (battery, motor, controller, charger, chassis).
	•	Multi-level: a line can be a sub-assembly with its own BOM (e.g. Vehicle → Chassis assembly → Welded frame → MS tubes). Exploded and indented views.
	•	Variants: one base BOM + variant overrides (colour, battery type, MS/SS frame, with/without charger) so 31 configurations do not need 31 full BOMs.
	•	Change control (ECN): any change to a released BOM = Engineering Change Note with reason, impact (open production orders, stock of old part), approval by Design Head + Plant Head → new revision.
	•	Where-used report for any component.
8.2 Routing BOM
	•	Ordered list of stages for the parent item: stage no., operation (e.g. 10 Frame welding, 20 Painting, 30 Wiring harness, 40 Final assembly, 50 Testing, 60 PDI), work centre, standard time (setup + run per unit), operators required, authorised operator skill, QC checkpoint (Y/N + checklist), in-house or job-work (vendor).
	•	Materials from the component BOM are tagged to the stage that consumes them, so issue can be stage-wise.
	•	Released and revised the same way as the component BOM; a routing revision is tied to a BOM revision.
8.3 Batch-wise production setup
	•	An item is marked batch-produced with a standard batch size (e.g. 10 vehicles). A production order is split into batches; each batch gets a batch no. that follows through MR, issue, stages, QC, PDI and the FG label.
8.4 Material Requisition against BOM
	•	From a released production order: requirement = BOM qty × order (or batch) qty × (1 + scrap %), exploded to the level the store issues at.
	•	Shown per line: required, already issued, returned, balance, free stock, shortage.
	•	MR can be raised for the whole order, per batch, or per stage (only the stage's materials).
	•	Shortage lines → one-click PR (§5.1). Alternate item can be issued only if listed on the BOM or approved by a deviation.
	•	Variance: actual issue − returns vs. BOM standard, per order and per batch.
9. Production — Production Order against SO, stage-wise and batch-wise
A Production Order (PO-P) is raised from an approved SO line (or for stock / sample), released only on a released BOM + routing, and progresses stage by stage, batch by batch, until finished goods pass PDI.
9.1 Production Order
	•	Sources: SO line (default), multiple SO lines of the same item consolidated, Make-to-stock (planner, with approval), Sample (§11), Rework.
	•	Fields: order no., item/variant, qty, SO ref(s) + customer, BOM revision, routing revision, planned start/end, priority, batch size → no. of batches, plant/line.
	•	Checks before release: BOM and routing released; material availability shown (available / shortage / on order with ETA); capacity per work centre shown. Release with shortage allowed only with Production Manager approval.
	•	Flow: Draft → Approved → Released → In progress → Completed → Closed (after FG receipt and variance review) / Short-closed (reason).
	•	On release: batches and job cards are generated; MR against BOM can be raised (§8.4).
9.2 Job cards and stage-wise booking
	•	One job card per batch per routing stage, in routing sequence. A stage can start only when the previous stage has output (or partial output, if allowed).
	•	Start / pause / complete with time stamps; operator(s) (only authorised for that stage), machine, shift.
	•	Output per stage: good qty, rework qty, rejected qty (reason), scrap; good qty moves to the next stage.
	•	Material consumption recorded at the stage that consumes it; serial-tracked parts scanned and bound to the unit (chassis no. ↔ motor, controller, battery, charger serials).
	•	Unit serial / chassis no. assigned at the first stage (or from a pre-printed series) and carried through.
	•	Job-work stage: stage sent to a vendor on a job-work challan (§5.3), received back, then continues.
	•	Process QC hold: if the stage has a QC checkpoint, output waits for PQC result before the next stage (§10.2).
	•	Rework loop: rejected units go to a rework job card and come back to the same stage; repeat counts tracked.
9.3 Completion and FG receipt
	•	Last stage output → PDI (§10.3) → PDI-passed units are received into the FG store against the order and reserved for the SO.
	•	Order closure shows: planned vs. actual qty, material variance (BOM vs. issued − returned), time variance (standard vs. actual hours), rejection %, cost per unit.
9.4 Shop-floor screens
	•	Production board: orders by status, batch progress across stages (Kanban), WIP by stage, today's plan vs. actual, delayed orders.
	•	Mobile/tablet: operator scans job card QR → start/complete → enter qty → scan serials.

11. Sample handling against the SO, with drawing approval
A sample is built only from an approved drawing revision, by authorised users, on a Sample Production Order linked to the enquiry or SO; the customer's approval of the sample is what lets the bulk SO go to production.
Flow
	•	Sample request from the enquiry or SO (Sales): item/spec, qty, required date, customer drawing/spec upload, chargeable or free.
	•	Drawing (Design/R&D): drawing no., revision (A, B, C…), file (PDF/DWG/image), linked item and BOM. Status: Draft → Internal review → Released for sample → Customer approved → Released for production / Superseded.
	•	Only drawing:create users upload; only drawing:approve users release (never the uploader).
	•	Every revision kept; production and purchase screens always show the current released revision, and a PO for a drawing-based part carries the drawing no. + revision.
	•	Watermark "UNCONTROLLED COPY" on prints of non-released revisions.
	•	Sample BOM / routing: may be a draft (trial) BOM, flagged "Sample"; allowed only on a Sample Production Order.
	•	Sample Production Order (§9, type Sample): MR → issue → stages → QC exactly as normal; costs captured separately.
	•	Sample QC / internal approval: QC + Design check the sample against the drawing; report attached.
	•	Sample dispatch: delivery challan (returnable or non-returnable), courier/AWB, date; approved by Sales Manager.
	•	Customer feedback: Approved / Approved with changes / Rejected, with remarks and files. Changes → new drawing revision → new sample cycle (sample iteration no. tracked).
	•	On approval: drawing and BOM are released for production (ECN if a released version existed); the SO lines referencing this sample become eligible for Production Orders.
Controls: SO lines marked "sample approval required" cannot raise a Production Order until the sample is customer-approved; sample stock sits in a separate Sample store; samples returned by the customer come back via gate entry (Returnable).
12. Dispatch and Sales Invoice
Only PDI-passed FG units reserved to an approved SO can be dispatched and invoiced, and each invoice line carries the unit's chassis and part serials into the warranty registry.
	•	Dispatch plan: SO lines ready (PDI passed, in FG store), customer, transporter, vehicle, planned date; approved by Dispatch In-charge.
	•	Packing / loading list: units picked by chassis no. scan; accessories, tool kit, manuals, PDI certificate, warranty card checked off.
	•	Credit and payment check: dispatch blocked if customer is over credit limit or advance terms are unpaid, unless Accounts/MD overrides with reason.
	•	Sales Invoice: created from the dispatch, never typed fresh: SO rate, GST (by place of supply), HSN, chassis/motor/controller/battery/charger serials per line, e-way bill no., e-invoice IRN/QR (via GSP integration or Tally). Number series per FY.
	•	Tally: invoice exported to Tally (XML/Excel) or Tally invoice imported back — to be decided (Questionnaire Q-INV). Today the CRM already imports Tally's Item Wise Sales Report into the service registry.
	•	Gate out: gate pass against invoice; vehicle out-time.
	•	Warranty registry: on invoice, each vehicle is registered in Service with its sale date and part serials (same snapshot rules as the existing Tally import), so service claims trace back to PO, MRN, IQC, production batch and PDI.
	•	SO closure: SO line closes when invoiced qty = ordered qty; order book updates.
Returns: customer returns come in via gate entry (Customer return) → IQC → credit note → stock to Rework / FG / Scrap.
12A. Accounting — complete accounts module and statutory compliance
Accounts is a complete double-entry module: every voucher — sales, purchase, payment, receipt, journal, expense, income, debit note and credit note — posts to the ledger, operational vouchers are built from the document before them, and statutory returns are prepared from the same books. Every voucher follows maker ≠ checker and value-based approval (§3).
12A.1 Sales Invoice (receivable)
	•	Created only from an approved dispatch (§12); lines, rate and GST copied from the SO; serials per line.
	•	Types: Tax invoice, Export/SEZ, Stock transfer (branch), Proforma (advance, no stock effect), Debit note, Credit note (against invoice: return, rate difference, discount — with approval).
	•	GST: CGST+SGST or IGST by place of supply, HSN summary, TCS where applicable, round-off; e-invoice IRN/QR and e-way bill.
	•	Status: Draft → Approved (Billing/Accounts) → Posted → Partly paid → Paid / Cancelled (with reason, before IRN cancel window closes).
	•	Customer ledger: invoice-wise outstanding, due date from payment terms, ageing (0–30, 31–60, 61–90, 90+), credit limit used.
12A.2 Purchase Invoice (payable)
	•	Booked against one or more approved MRNs (§6) — never against a PO alone — so only accepted qty is paid for.
	•	Three-way match: PO rate × MRN accepted qty vs. vendor invoice. Differences above a tolerance (₹ or %) hold the invoice for Purchase Manager approval with reason.
	•	Fields: vendor invoice no. + date (duplicate blocked per vendor), GSTIN, GST input (CGST/SGST/IGST, ITC eligible Y/N), TDS section and rate, freight/other charges, due date.
	•	Debit note auto-proposed for rejected qty (IQC) and short receipt.
	•	Also for expenses/services without MRN (service PO or direct expense) — with HOD approval.
	•	Status: Draft → Verified (Accounts) → Approved → Posted → Partly paid → Paid.
	•	Vendor ledger: invoice-wise payable, due date, ageing, advances paid.
12A.3 Payment and Receipt
	•	Payment to vendor: against one or more purchase invoices (or as an advance against a PO); mode (NEFT/RTGS/cheque/UPI/cash), bank account, UTR/cheque no., TDS deducted; maker–checker approval with value limits (§3); payment advice emailed to the vendor.
	•	Receipt from customer: against invoices (bill-wise allocation) or as an advance against an SO; mode, UTR, bank; unallocated amount kept as on-account; TDS by customer recorded.
	•	Bank reconciliation: upload bank statement (Excel/CSV), auto-match by UTR/amount/date, manual match for the rest.
	•	Due lists: vendor payments due this week; customer collections due/overdue — linked to the CRM so the sales owner sees their customer's overdue before a new SO.
	•	Cash payments above a limit blocked (Income Tax s.40A(3) / 269ST checks).
12A.4 Journal Voucher
	•	For entries with no other voucher: provisions, accruals, depreciation, adjustments, rectifications, inter-ledger transfers, opening balances.
	•	Multi-line debit/credit; must balance; cost centre per line; narration mandatory; attachments.
	•	Recurring journals (monthly rent accrual, depreciation) with a schedule; reversing journals (auto-reverse on the 1st of next month).
	•	Contra voucher for cash ↔ bank and bank ↔ bank transfers.
	•	Approval: Accounts Manager; above limit, Finance Head / MD.
12A.5 Expense — direct and indirect
	•	Direct expenses (go to cost of production / trading): freight inward, job-work charges, power and fuel for the plant, factory wages, carriage, packing material, consumables. Can be linked to a Production Order or batch so it lands in product cost.
	•	Indirect expenses (go to P&L): rent, salaries (office), travel, telephone, advertising, professional fees, repairs, bank charges, office expenses, depreciation.
	•	Expense booking: vendor/party (or employee for reimbursement), expense ledger, cost centre / department, GST input (eligible or blocked under s.17(5)), TDS section, RCM flag, bill upload.
	•	Employee expense claims and petty cash: claim → HOD approval → Accounts → payment; petty cash imprest with daily cash book.
	•	Budget per expense head and cost centre; warning or block when exceeded.
12A.6 Income — direct and indirect
	•	Direct income: sales of vehicles, spares and services, job-work income, scrap sales (from Scrap store, with GST/TCS).
	•	Indirect income: interest received, discount received, rent received, commission, insurance claims, foreign exchange gain, misc. income.
	•	Booked through a sales invoice (if GST-able) or an income voucher; TDS deducted by the payer recorded for Form 26AS/AIS matching.
12A.7 Debit Note
	•	Against purchase (to a vendor): item-wise (rejection, short supply — links to IQC/GRN, reduces stock via purchase return) or amount-only (rate difference, late-delivery penalty, quality claim) — with GST reversal where applicable.
	•	Against sales (to a customer): amount-only for a price increase, freight or interest recovery, short-billing — a supplementary invoice with GST.
	•	Others: against a party with no invoice (penalties, recoveries), via journal-style lines.
	•	Must reference the original invoice no. + date for GST; approval mandatory; reason code mandatory.
12A.8 Credit Note
	•	Against sales (to a customer): item-wise (sales return — goods back via gate entry and IQC, stock to Rework/FG/Scrap) or amount-only (discount, rate difference, warranty/claim settlement, scheme) — with GST reversal, e-invoice IRN for B2B credit notes.
	•	Against purchase (from a vendor): record the vendor's credit note — item-wise or amount-only — reducing payable.
	•	Others: against a party with no invoice.
	•	Original invoice reference, reason code, approval; GST time limit check (by 30 November after the FY end).
12A.9 Accounting masters, books and statements
	•	Chart of accounts: groups (Indian Schedule III layout: assets, liabilities, income, expense), sub-groups, ledgers; party ledgers auto-created for customers and vendors; bank and cash ledgers; GST, TDS, TCS duty ledgers.
	•	Cost centres / profit centres: plant, department, product line, branch; every voucher line can carry one.
	•	Financial years and periods: period lock after closing (only Finance Head may unlock, with reason, audited); opening balances import.
	•	Fixed assets: asset register, capitalisation from purchase invoice, depreciation per Companies Act (WDV/SLM) and Income Tax block rates.
	•	Inventory valuation: weighted average or FIFO; stock value in the balance sheet from the stock ledger; WIP and FG valued at standard or actual cost.
	•	Books and reports: day book, cash book, bank book, ledger, sub-ledger, trial balance, profit and loss, balance sheet (Schedule III), cash flow, receivables/payables ageing, bill-wise outstanding, cost-centre P&L, budget vs actual, interest calculation on overdue.
	•	Multi-currency for imports/exports with exchange-rate gain/loss.
