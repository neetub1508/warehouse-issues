1. PRODUCT SCOPE AND MODULE LIST
================================================================
The capabilities page lists these modules, each with a one-line description [CAP]:
- Forecasting: "Uses statistical algorithms, IoT data, and other information to develop the most accurate forecasts possible for every level of your supply chain."
- Multi-Echelon Optimization (MEO): "Determines the lowest cost solution to achieve desired service levels across a multi-echelon supply chain using advanced optimization solvers."
- Asset Sustainability Optimization (ASO): "Develops stocking plans to guarantee desired uptime or availability levels of complex assets such as aircraft, weapons systems, or utilities."
- Network Optimization: "Determines the optimal stocking locations and recommends coverage for the installed base to minimize facility and transportation costs while meeting SLA requirements."
- Lifecycle Analysis: supports "new product introductions, end-of-life, and configuration changes."
- Connected SPM: Servigistics combined with ThingWorx, "collecting data directly from your install base."
- Initial Provisioning: "Sets procurement and stocking recommendations for parts associated with new products or product families."
- K-curve Cycle Stock: "Determine order quantity for order planning by developing an optimal ABC classification of parts by order frequency, including annual usage, part/pair cost and order frequency sets."
- Last Time Buy: "Systematically mines lifecycle demand data from parts that have been through the entire lifecycle."
- Demand Planning: covers "low-volume and sporadic demand, new product introduction and end-of-life, and a rapidly changing install base with frequent engineering changes."
- Lifecycle Management: "Forecast parts demand for every stage of a product's lifecycle."
- Dealer Inventory Management: "Empowers your dealers to maintain optimal stocking levels."
- Two resellers (INAS in Romania, RAM Systems in Saudi Arabia) copy the same module list. RAM adds "forecasting billions of part locations every day" and "trusted by US Air Force, US Coast Guard, US Navy, US Army, Boeing, Airbus" [PARTNER].

The April 2023 Stanford lecture groups the suite into five blocks [STAN]:
- Demand Forecasting: "Predict parts demands using history, equipment and maintenance data including predictions for new and last time buy parts using machine learning."
- Inventory Optimization: "AI optimized stocking levels using a predictive twin of the supply chain."
- Order Planning & Planner Workbench: "Optimize return, repair, transfer and reuse of existing assets to ensure a procurement averse and sustainable service supply chain."
- Pricing: "Optimize and manage service parts' prices globally incorporating rules and competitive data."
- Advanced Analytics & Modeling: "Continuous improvement with performance analytics, ML based root cause analysis, what-if modeling and simulation."

Gartner's Magic Quadrant for Supply Chain Planning (as quoted by PTC) says: "Servigistics provides advanced capabilities in forecasting, network and multi-echelon supply chain optimization, asset sustainability, demand planning, dealer inventory management and more." [GART]

The product-page FAQ gives a longer list of differentiators, citing Blumberg and IDC: "multi-echelon optimization and multi-part optimization, complex substitution logic, advanced order planning logic, Last Time Buy, performance analytics and intelligence, and mature AI/ML-powered algorithms." [PROD]

The same FAQ says ERP falls short on service-specific needs: "complex substitution logic, return and repair capabilities, and Last Time Buy requirements." [PROD]

Licence metrics [SAAS]. There are three pricing bases, plus a sizing limit:
- PMI (inventory under management): "sold in blocks of US$1 million". Everything counts: "All parts, at all locations… regardless of the condition of the inventory (e.g., on hand new, on hand fixed, on hand bad, on order, in return, in repair)."
- PXL: part numbers × locations. The example given is "10,000 part numbers and 4 locations… 40,000 PXLs".
- DAL: "Servigistics SaaS Retail Inventory Management (RIM) for OEM". It is licensed by "Dealer Assigned Locations (DAL) to which the Customer provides a log-in". A DAL is "identifiable by street address". A dealer may use it only to manage that OEM's parts, "not the service parts of any other OEM". It is not sold to customers "who sell through only a small number of dealers."
- PLP (part/location pairs) is a fourth variable used as a sizing limit: "In SPM forecasting and planning are done for each part at each location where it has been used in the past (demand) or is anticipated to be used in the future (forecast)."

3. FORECASTING (functional detail)
================================================================
Three demand drivers [HV]:
- "independent and random demand forecasted by blending demand history with causal factors and failure rates"
- "IoT data taken directly from assets"
- "scheduled demand calculated by using maintenance schedules and associated bills of materials"
- PTC summarizes: "Servigistics applies sophisticated, proprietary algorithms to produce a comprehensive view of which parts are needed along with quantity and location."

Causal and installed-base forecasting:
- Hitachi Vantara: "incorporate install base as a significant portion of our demand generator, so we can look at a product in various lifecycle phases." Causal forecasting also captures battery shelf life so they can "move or retire batteries before going bad" [HV].
- Causal Forecasting "computes failure rates based on historical failures, applying them in a metric scaled by the fleet and its activity profile (hours and cycles), and install base." It can compute "a matrix of MTBUR values, which could vary by geography, application of the asset, the age of the equipment, or the asset in which the part is installed" [TR24].
- Machine-learning stratification (Steven Caldwell): the system will "interrogate historical demand for the part globally… determine which attributes, and signals are significant in predicting, a deviation from the average… stratify the demand accordingly and calculate the failure rate at the right level of granularity" [TR24].
- MTBF estimation [STAN]: "Forecasted MTBF/Failure rate using statistical and ML algorithms". It handles "Part and Bill of Materials changes over time", "Cutover from Engineering to Forecasted MTBF", "New Part MTBF prediction", and "Product and Region specific MTBF".
- Installed-base data can also set "optimization service targets specific to contract/customer/product" [STAN].

Connected (IoT) forecasting:
- Uses ThingWorx data to "predict the removal of Life Limited Parts' (LLPs), rotables, and maintenance events" from "the status of serialized components" [CIMCF].
- The same release added "new forecasting algorithms to enable improved repair forecasting, new optimization features including space capacity optimization, new performance-based logistics management features" [CIMCF].
- The FAQ lists three IoT uses: "Develop causal forecasts based on asset population and utilization data; Schedule serialized life-limited parts removals; Adjust stocking plans to reflect changes to your install base" [SPMTECH].
- Stanford lecture: installed-base utilization ("flight hours, cycles") drives unscheduled removals, while serialized utilization drives rotable and life-limited-part removals [STAN].
- On data timing, Wodarski writes: "If you run demand forecasts on a monthly basis, all you need to do is pull equipment utilization data generated over the past 30 days." Real-time data is not needed because "parts planners don't run forecasts 24/7." He also suggests forecasting the connected segment of the installed base separately ("Segment A") [FCST].

Special forecast types:
- "specialized methods for low and sporadic demand, cluster analysis to support the launch and end-of-life forecasts, and, by extension Initial Provisioning and Last Time Buy recommendations" [AI24].
- Blumberg: "unique models for low-volume and sporadic demand and can break the reliance on historical data through the installed base, causal, and IoT connected forecasts" [BLUM].
- NedTrain: "Forecast separately for planned and unplanned usage" [NED].
- Embraer: "forecast different streams of demand" [EMB].
- KONE: "better determine which components fall into the unplanned demand streams" [KONE].
- USAF requirement: "independent (unscheduled) and dependent (scheduled) forecasts, including the expert selection of forecasting algorithms, and the application of causal factors such as flying hours" [USAFB].

Release-specific forecasting items:
- Legacy MCA users gained "more options on modeling demand statistics (scaled or de-trended standard deviation and forecast error)" and "more flexible optimization groups" [PW].
- 13.1 (May 2024), per the ARC summary: "Machine Learning-based forecast algorithms are now fully integrated"; a "new forecast parameter… to help reduce churn in forecast method changes"; "top contributing locations" shown in the Forecast Worksheet [ARC131, UNVERIFIED wording].
- 14.0 (July 2025): "multivariate and MTBF methodologies"; "consensus forecasting for SIOP" [R14].
- Spring 2026: "Advanced ML models continuously improve forecast accuracy by identifying complex demand patterns, causal relationships, and changing service behaviors" [PROD].

Pre-PTC behaviour (Cray, about 2002) [CRAY]:
- Forecasts "every part at every location", from history (inventory/CRM) plus projections such as MTBF.
- Replans nightly: "Every night, the system looks at what occurred during the day, and comes up with new numbers the next day."
- Corrects engineering failure rates: "We have 14 of these parts out here, and engineering said one would fail every year, but two are failing every year."

4. INVENTORY OPTIMIZATION (MEO, MIME, availability)
================================================================
How PTC defines MEO [MEOvMEP]:
- MEO sets inventory "based on the relationships between all the locations across your network and all of the parts at the lowest cost".
- It considers "effective lead times between stocking locations… overall service level… demand for all parts… budget constraints."
- The algorithm "computes the marginal contribution to level of service for each dollar of inventory 'spent'."
- It uses a "matrix of availability targets for part groups and location types."
- Stock can sit "in higher echelons where it has higher utilization, and rebalancing and pooling logic manages a dynamic network."
- "Critical slow moving parts are assigned high levels of service."
- It supports strategic modeling of "opening or closing locations, rerouting physical flows, collapsing echelons, imposing budget constraints."
- By contrast, multi-echelon planning just uses "individual fill rates you set for each of those locations." PTC's worked example: 1 global warehouse, 11 regional warehouses, 28 forward stocking locations.

Wodarski's definition: "calculate every part at every location for the entire planning horizon in one run… comprehend… budget and space constraints." Things that can be modeled include "Forecasts, lead times, service goals, reverse material flows for reparable parts, network structure, and the size and configuration of the supported fleet." [TR24]

Claimed benefit: "same service levels with up to 35% less inventory than traditional ERP planning" [MEOSC].

Objectives and constraints (IDC 2023–24, Aly Pinder):
- The optimizer handles "availability/product uptime, multi-period, budget, sustainability objectives, space constraints, referral location, churn control, dealers/retail inventory, contract type, multi-indenture, and rotables."
- It includes "comprehensive scenario based what-if modeling" [IDC24].

Blumberg on MEO [BLUM]:
- MEO evaluates "multiple service level agreement (SLA) / performance-based logistics (PBL) commitments, customer wait time, fill rates."
- Strategic modeling covers "forecast, service goals, installed base, network configuration and cost, and cube constraints."
- Optimization modes: single item, MEO, "Multi-Indenture/Multi-Echelon (MIME), and asset availability optimization."
- Parts Grouping and Parts Relationship: planners can group "by any type of part attribute, including cost, criticality, demand volumes… no limit to the number of criteria"; "part/location groups… (e.g., central stores vs. branch or truck stock)."
- The algorithms were validated with Muckstadt (Cornell) and Cohen (Wharton), and vetted by LMI, Capgemini, Boeing and Lockheed Martin.

Other optimization detail:
- Stanford math framing: "Probabilistic Twin, Markov chains, Monte-Carlo Simulations" to predict; "Heuristics for Stochastic NLIP" to decide "Stocking Levels, Repair, Buy, Move" [STAN].
- Stanford "maturity curve", release by release: Release 9 = item-optimized, business-rule based; Release 10 = multi-item/multi-echelon, forward and reverse, multisource, contract fill rate and response time; Release 11 = contract/product availability and uptime, new buy/repair, investment, churn control, space, multi-indenture; Release 12+ = connected/predictive [STAN].
- Differentiators on the same slides: "Optimize entire supply chain in single model (OEM+Dealers)"; "Multi-period optimization for seasonality and part supersessions" [STAN].
- IDC 2025: "time-phased MEO engine… accurately modeling fulfillment paths"; validation through "Monte Carlo simulation, blind tests, and predictive twin analysis" [IDC25].
- QKS: "multi-echelon optimization with MIME" [QKS].
- A&D: "performance-based logistics (PBLs) contracts with readiness-based sparing (RBS)" [PROD]. PBL and SLA contracts are supported "from inception through sustainment through retirement" [PROD].
- USAF requirement: "achieve specified weapon system availability while simultaneously minimizing inventory investment and adhering to financial constraints, combining Readiness Based Sparing (RBS) principles, aircraft uptime and fill rate optimization" [USAFB].
- Timeline: "First defense Multi-Indenture Multi-Echelon (MIME)/Readiness-Based Sparing (RBS) commercial solution" and "4th-Generation multi-echelon optimization" [LEG].

Service-level targets by part class (Cray example):
- For cheap, critical parts in a new product, a user "might require a fill rate of 99.8 percent", but "a 70-percent fill rate might be acceptable" for parts over $1,000 in an older product.
- The model includes pipelines for returned and repaired parts: "How long does it take to get the bad one back, then how long does it take to repair it?" [CRAY]

Initial provisioning:
- USCG achieved "accurate, cost-effective initial provisioning for its air bases" [TR24].

Simulation:
- Thermo Fisher used "advanced simulation capabilities to identify the most critical parts" [TR24].
- Southwest Airlines uses "Enhanced Supply Chain Modeling (ESCM) and Multi-Echelon Optimization" [TR24].
- The "stochastic digital twin" is used "to simulate and predict model uncertainties, disruptions" [PROD].

5. ORDER / SUPPLY PLANNING AND PLANNER WORKFLOW
================================================================
Supply sequencing:
- "The supply plan efficiently sequences through the sourcing options to maximize the utilization of assets already owned and consequently minimize the requirements for new part orders" [BLUM]. PTC calls this "procurement-averse" [GART][STAN].

Work queue and exceptions:
- "The management by exception criteria… allowing the system to do the bulk of the planning… issues are presented in a prioritized work queue that is re-evaluated each day" [BLUM].
- The user interface lets a planner "see everything within one layered screen" [BLUM].
- USAF requirement: "error reporting and event management capabilities to alert users… and make autonomous recommendations to reconcile exceptions within discrete planner work queues for demand planning, inventory optimization and supply planning" [USAFB].
- USAF distribution planning: "correlate repair, purchase and (re)distribution orders with current or future demands" [USAFB].

Order Planning at KONE:
- "apply specific, scenario-based optimization rules for different types of parts, thus automating certain actions and approvals and improving supplier scheduling processes" [KONE].

ERP loop at Metso:
- SAP sends master and transactional data into Servigistics.
- Servigistics "sends purchase requests right back to SAP."
- SAP turns them into "purchase or stock-transfer orders" [MET].

Other planning capabilities from case studies:
- Embraer used it for "complex interchangeability relationships, rebalance inventory across a global network, create location and part types with associated attributes… automate its inventory replenishment process" [EMB].
- NedTrain can "Identify exceptions, investigate the issues behind them, and take corrective action." It manages central and regional stock across 9 main locations, 35 service locations and 65 warehouses [NED].
- Qantas: stocking policies are set by part "classification (i.e. cost, usage, essentiality)." Users "model probabilistic maintenance bills of materials (BOMs), and rebalance materials" [QAN].
- 13.1 supply planning, per the ARC summary: vendor "monthly limits", "multiple vendors… in priority order", and guidance for "resolving shortages, vendor issues, and demand misses" [ARC131, UNVERIFIED].
- 14.0: "the most sophisticated order planning engine with additional features and repair optimization" [R14].
- Aviation panel point: sites reorder the same part independently at different prices, so the system aggregates demand and coordinates purchases across the network [MEOSC].
- Accenture/OnProcess connectors feed "field data, returns, repairs, and forward stocking location visibility" into Servigistics [MEOSC].

Planner training tasks (a proxy for daily work):
- "reviewing forecast exceptions, adjusting optimization parameters, and interpreting system recommendations" [TRAIN].
- Axcelis mentions a "unified dashboard view" [TRAIN].

Job-posting evidence (search snippets only, UNVERIFIED as quoted text):
- Planners "optimize inventory, reorder points, safety stocks and ordering frequency".
- "Converting demand into a supply plan and vendor forecasts".
- "Ensuring ASL, Servigistics and Material Master Files are monitored, maintained, and updated" [JOBS].
- Specialist roles ask for "Demand & Supply Planning, Oracle Order Management… Reverse Logistics" [JOBS].

9. SERVICE PARTS PRICING (SPP)
================================================================
General:
- SPP "can be integrated with parts planning or used standalone." It "shares the Servigistics database." Named users: GM, Komatsu, Nissan [BLUM].

General Motors [GM]:
- Scope: 1.5M parts, up to 8M SKUs, 72 warehouses.
- "automatically price the 80 percent of parts that produce 20 percent of revenue… focus… on the 20 percent… that generate 80 percent of revenue."
- Result: "Turn over 3% global revenue ($30M)".
- Rollout: pilot in Dubai in December 2009, then regional instances to be joined into one global data view. Middleware was used to clean data from 120 source applications.

JLG [JLG]:
- 140,000 parts. Moved from cost-plus pricing, with category-specific strategies: "Each category of parts requires a unique pricing strategy, as well as different rules and workflows."
- Inputs considered: competitor set, price leader vs follower, perceived value, volume, positioning, profitability.
- Exception-based: about 20% of parts get local input and about 80% are priced automatically.
- Only 29% of parts were under SPP management at first, and these were compared against the rest.
- ROI in under 12 months. The three-year value came in 60% above projection.

SAIC-GM [SAIC]:
- Upgraded from version 9.44 to 11.2 starting September 2016.
- "part chain" pricing: newer versions supersede old parts.
- "Table-Driven Curve Pricing" lets experts "explore the relationship between cost and margin percentages." It uses cost, price, sales history and markup factor per part/type/family, with custom tables linked to pricing rules.
- Approval workflows are connected to email, so prices are "approved and released to dealers."
- Pricing became 20–30% faster.

Release items:
- 13.1: "generate new prices based on user-defined offsets"; kit pricing uses "all price buckets" and a "review reason to alert users of outdated part kit prices" [ARC131, UNVERIFIED].
- 14.0: "dealer- and customer-specific simulations, feedback integration, and AI-enabled workflows" [R14].

11. SERVICEMAX INTEGRATION ("Field Stock Optimization")
================================================================
- First released in ServiceMax Core 23R3. Servigistics recommends target stock levels "visible in ServiceMax for technician vans and other forward stocking locations." Users "can view and adjust stock levels directly in ServiceMax Core" [SMX23]. Integration announced January 2024 [TR24].
- Inputs pulled from ServiceMax: "demand details, parts master data, product replacement information, current inventory levels, and inventory locations." Output: "optimized inventory levels by location and product" [SMX-FSO].

Functional requirements [SMX-FR]:
- Extracts from the Asset 360 standard data model.
- "Bidirectional data movement".
- Transformation into the target format.
- "full or incremental data export".
- Custom fields may override standard ones.
- Multi-tenant: "Data separation across customers", with separately scheduled export jobs.

Data flows [SMX-FL]:
- Mechanism: Salesforce to AWS AppFlow to S3 as CSV, one file per execution, named by execution ID. "You must create eight flows for each integration. Seven… to get data from ServiceMax… and one flow to insert and/or update the parts planning engine output back."
- DemandDetail: "daily consumption of material so it can calculate failure rates".
- LocMaster: inventory locations "such as DC, FSL, Tech".
- PartChainDetail: substitutions, e.g. "If part A is not available, then Part A1 can be used as A1 is backwards compatible".
- PartMaster: may come from ERP instead, for "cost, weight, size, lead time".
- Product: only the top-level serviced product under contract; "inherently tied to the Contract feed".
- StockAmount: current stock levels.
- Outbound flow: "stocklevel". Key constraint: "Required Qty is a formula field so Reorder Qty and Reorder Point must be utilized for integration."
- UNVERIFIED inference: the output lands as reorder point and reorder quantity (min/max style) on ServiceMax product stock. Only six inbound flows are named; the seventh is probably a Contract feed.

Other ServiceMax material:
- Webcast topics included the "Future parts management integration road map" [SMXWC].
- The June 2026 ServiceMax release adds "smarter parts handling" [NEWS26].
- ServiceMax AI can answer "smarter parts queries" [NEWS25].
